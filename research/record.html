Research · The record · 26 October 2026

The full record

Every experiment we have run, as our ledger recorded it: what held, what did not, what could not answer its own question, and what we got wrong. Each one had its decision rule written before its code. Numbers in brackets are 95% intervals.

In short

  • Correct signal teaches; noise does not. Checked reward taught a 1.5B model a skill it lacked; coin-flip reward, run identically, broke it.
  • Checkers leak, measurably. On MBPP, 17.9% of problems accept a solution we can show is broken, and the evaluation harness alone moves one model from 0.608 to 0.460.
  • Most of what we tried did not work, and the nulls are below with the same detail as the wins: self-generated data, harder curricula, reflection without weight updates, and a learned referee all failed their own tests.
  • We caught our own errors before believing them — a fake train/test split, a control that replayed the treatment, verdicts that contradicted their numbers. Those are listed too.
  • Many runs use small models trained from scratch (4.9M parameters) on generated tasks; the scale of each is stated. Audits of other groups' work appear here once the authors have had time to reply.

What held

Effects whose interval excludes zero under the rule written for them, or measurements we stand behind.

ExperimentWhat we askedWhat came outScale and limits
Only correct signal teaches Does reward from a code-running checker teach a skill the model lacks, and is it the correctness that teaches? Checked reward vs untrained: +0.100 [+0.040, +0.170]. Coin-flip reward: −0.050 [−0.100, −0.010]. No transfer to GSM8K. Qwen2.5-1.5B, one seed, 60 steps; depth is nominal (see the correction on its page).
External proposer and verifier Is the "scale threshold" for self-improvement really a threshold on proposing and checking one's own problems? External proposer and verifier beat self-proposing and self-verifying in both runs: +0.555 [+0.523, +0.586] and +0.235 [+0.203, +0.266]. 4.9M-parameter model, generated tasks. Which half binds came out opposite in the two runs: unresolved.
Verified handoffs Can a solver that is perfect on short problems reach long ones by chaining checked steps? At depth 8: chain 1.000 vs single model 0.033, +0.967 [+0.917, +1.000]. But an unchecked chain did as well: the gain is the split, not the check. 4.9M-parameter model, generated tasks.
Curriculum buys depth Does training one composition level deeper buy extrapolation to depths never seen? Yes, by the pre-registered rule: curriculum buys depth. 4.9M-parameter model; both arms in one session, same tokenizer and size.
Compounding Does a self-training loop keep compounding across generations? It compounds for about two generations, then plateaus without collapsing. 5 generations, 3 arms; drift control clean.
Held-out primitives Does the loop learn something that spans building blocks, or a bag of them? It transfers across primitives (+0.116). Small model, generated tasks.
Forgetting tracks memorisation Does forgetting follow model capacity, or how much was memorised? Replicated on three independent draws: forgetting rises with memorisation (r +0.91 to +0.94); retention stays near 0.08–0.10 at every rank. Sign agreement is the claim; no pooled interval.
The harness moves the score How much does the evaluation scaffold alone change a score? Same weights, same 250 problems, two defensible scaffolds: 0.608 vs 0.460, against a noise floor of 0.088. One checkpoint, no training.
Benchmarks accept broken solutions How often does a public code benchmark accept a solution that is wrong? MBPP: 17.9% of problems (169 of 942) [15.6, 20.5]. 95.2% of flagged MBPP problems are confirmed by EvalPlus's independent tests. Single-site broken versions only, so this is a floor on gameability, not an estimate.
Repairing a test suite Do the asserts that catch broken versions produce a genuinely stronger suite? They remove 61% of held-out gameability — not the 100% our first, buggy aggregation claimed. 60 generated problems at depth 4.
How far hardening goes Is there a floor on how clean repeated hardening can make a suite? Held-out survival floors at 0.009 at 24 or more tests; the floor is set by suite size, not by rounds. 53 problems scorable at every round.
Erosion with depth Does test-based checking degrade as problems compose? Survival of broken solutions rose from depth 2 to 6 (+0.037 [+0.017, +0.057]) and then stopped. The 0.056 plateau was a 10-test artefact: with 44 tests the same measurement gives 0.004. Generated problems, depths 2–10.
A gate with judgement Can a gate that accepts or rejects self-generated data beat a random gate making the same number of updates? The first gate in our work to beat a rate-matched control: +0.065 [+0.005, +0.120]. One seed, marginal; the rule as written did not award "has judgement".
A noisy verifier does damage What happens to self-training when the verifier is wrong 15% of the time? Measurable harm: 18 problems lost, 4 gained, net −14; interval excludes zero. One of the two arms completed; the other died in its final evaluation.
Properties vs examples Can a verifier built from properties of the spec replace example tests? No, as pre-registered: at 16 clauses, properties leave 71.1% of broken versions alive against 4.5% for 16 example tests. 150 generated problems, depths 3–5.

What did not work

Nulls and negative results, each against its own pre-registered rule.

ExperimentWhat we askedWhat came outScale and limits
A perfect verifierDoes self-training on perfectly verified data raise capability? No measurable change over six generations: clean self-generated data neither helped nor hurt.150 held-out MBPP problems.
A gate that "helped"Did gating noisy self-generated data help because of its judgement? It beat no gate (+0.1200) but not a random gate making as few updates: the benefit was fewer updates.Same setup, 35% verifier error.
A gate that never acceptsDoes that gate accept clean data? It rejected 6 of 6 generations even with a perfect verifier: it was a brake, not a judge.Free Colab.
Test-time trainingDoes test-time training adapt the model, or select among answers it already had? Selection, not adaptation, at both depths — overturned by our own control library after a first, more positive reading.Small model.
Training on its own compositionsCan a loop supply its own frontier by composing problems it can solve? First design: worse, −0.460 [−0.530, −0.385]. Second design: no gain, +0.065 [−0.020, +0.150].4.9M-parameter model; that line was stopped.
Harder problemsDoes escalating difficulty beat a fixed curriculum? It hurt: −0.124 [−0.156, −0.093] against the fixed arm at the target depth.30 generations, 3 arms.
Capacity or representationDoes more capacity, up to nearly ten times the parameters, raise the score at depths the model was not trained on? No: a representational limit, by the rule written before any arm ran.Four arms; only capacity varied.
Reflection without weight updatesDoes an agent that reflects on feedback, weights frozen, improve beyond a no-feedback control? No: mean gain 6.33, below the pre-set bar of 10.100 held-out games per arm.
A learned refereeCan a model be trained into a calibrated judge of correctness? Useless signal: discrimination (AUROC) below 0.6 in every domain.Colab T4.
A 1.5B model on its own compositionsDoes a real 1.5B model climb a depth ladder by training on its own verified compositions? The loop starved: the model could not produce enough verified compositions at its frontier; nothing moved on any held-out measure.LoRA fine-tuning at a small dose.
A 1.5B pilot of externalisationDoes the external-proposer result hold at 1.5B? Uninformative: the treatment arm itself did not move (+0.007 [−0.033, +0.047]), so all arms were compared at zero.Pilot, 3 generations.
Predicting reward hackingDoes our model-free audit predict which problems a real model will pass with wrong code? Inconclusive at stage 1.Free Colab T4.
A world model's action signalDoes conditioning on the action improve prediction? Marginal to negative: the action is decodable, but using it did not measurably help.Phase 0.
Overshoot and forgettingDoes overshoot cause catastrophic forgetting? Refuted.Earlier study.
A plasticity breakIs there an adapter rank below which transfer breaks? No break down to rank 1: plasticity is not the binding constraint here.One seed.

What we got wrong, and caught

Every one of these was found before the result was believed, and fixed in the code that produced it.

  • A fake train/test split. Two "independent" halves of a hardening test saw the same broken versions; the held-out control was decorative. Found by reading the code.
  • Survivorship in an average. Problems that finished early dropped out of later rounds, so the average was taken over a shrinking set. It had reported a perfect repair.
  • A zero read as missing. A survival of exactly zero was treated as no data.
  • A control that replayed the treatment. A random-gate control reused the gated arm's choices, giving a difference of exactly zero with a zero-width interval.
  • Verdicts that contradicted their own numbers. One printed "refuted" when its rule was not met; another said "interval spans zero" above an interval that did not. Verdict text is now generated from the same checks it prints.
  • A silent no-op. A text substitution matched nothing and scored a perfect model at zero.
  • A confound in our earlier work from the tokenizer, measured and recorded.
  • Our own headline, partly inflated. When we re-checked our benchmark flags against independent tests, 85.7% (MBPP) and 78.6% (HumanEval) were confirmed, short of our 95% bar. The next version raised that to 95.2% and 84.6%, and the public numbers use it.
  • A mis-stated checker certification on our first research page; corrected on the page.

Not listed here yet: our audits of other groups' public claims. Each author hears from us privately first, and the audit appears here once they have replied or two weeks have passed.
